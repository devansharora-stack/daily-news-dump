AI Risk

Stable Miscalibration in Large Language Models: High-Confidence Errors That Persist
arXiv AI
Researchers found that LLMs exhibit 'stable miscalibration' — confidently wrong answers that remain wrong even under perturbation, rather than being fragile flukes. This has major implications for enterprise deployments where AI outputs are consumed as authoritative, as it suggests high-confidence errors may be systematically harder to detect and filter than previously assumed.
Never the Number: Why AI text-to-SQL systems produce fluent wrong answers
arXiv AI
Research demonstrates that LLM-powered text-to-SQL systems generate wrong but fluent answers that are indistinguishable from correct ones to end users — a critical risk for enterprise dashboards and BI tools. The paper argues for 'structural abstention' where systems refuse to answer rather than risk silent errors, directly addressing the Hallucination Tax problem in enterprise AI.
Circuit-level AI interpretability evidence does not survive defensible analytic variation
arXiv AI
A pre-registered study found that mechanistic interpretability results — the kind of evidence the EU AI Act requires for high-risk AI documentation — change substantially when two competent analysts use the same tools with different but defensible settings. This undermines a key compliance pathway for enterprises deploying high-risk AI systems in Europe.
No Universal Signal Predicts Sample-Level LLM Regression under Version Updates
arXiv AI
Research shows there is no reliable way to predict which individual queries will break when an LLM is updated to a newer version — even when aggregate performance improves. For enterprises running production AI systems, this means model upgrades carry unpredictable regression risks that standard testing may not catch, requiring more robust monitoring strategies.
