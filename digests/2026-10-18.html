AI Ethics & Trust

Meta removes language that raised scraping and AI training concerns
TechCrunch AI
After significant backlash, Meta updated its commercial terms to remove language that appeared to grant itself broad rights to scrape content from websites — including news sites — for AI training. The reversal highlights the ongoing tension between AI companies' insatiable data needs and content owners' intellectual property rights, a flashpoint that could shape future licensing deals and regulation.

Bias Control and Injection for Multi-Agent Aggregation
arXiv AI
Research reveals that when multiple AI agents disagree, their collective decisions are susceptible to non-semantic biases like majority pressure and response-style preferences that can override quality reasoning. This has direct implications for enterprises deploying multi-agent architectures for decision-making — without bias controls, these systems may amplify rather than correct individual model weaknesses.

Agentic AI

Websites are closing their doors to AI agents
The Verge AI
Users of AI agents from Meta, xAI, OpenAI, and Instinct are reporting widespread blocks from top websites including Yelp, preventing agents from completing tasks like booking reservations or making purchases. This emerging friction threatens the core value proposition of agentic AI — autonomous task completion — and signals that the internet infrastructure is not ready for an agent-driven economy. Companies building agentic strategies need to consider access as a critical bottleneck.
Agent Status Protocol (ASP): A Lightweight Specification for State Reporting in Multi-Agent Systems
arXiv AI
Researchers propose a standardized protocol for AI agents to report their operational state — including detection of crashed, hung, or compromised agents that can't self-report. As enterprises deploy multi-agent systems, the lack of observability standards has been a major governance gap; this specification addresses agent monitoring, a prerequisite for production-grade agentic deployments.
AutoOnboarder: Autonomously Onboarding LLM Agents to New Tasks
arXiv AI
Research confirms that LLM agents lose reasoning capability when placed inside operational harnesses (the software that mediates their actions). AutoOnboarder addresses this by having a supervisor agent adapt the agent to each task, similar to onboarding a new employee. This finding is critical for enterprises experiencing disappointing agent performance — the deployment infrastructure itself may be degrading AI capabilities.
Evolving Together, Failing Apart: The Skill-Policy Co-evolution Trap in Self-Improving Agents
arXiv AI
Researchers identify a fundamental failure mode in self-improving AI agents: when an agent's skill library is updated after deployment but the policy model isn't retrained to match, performance degrades significantly. This 'co-evolution trap' has direct implications for enterprises maintaining agentic systems — it means AI agents require continuous, coordinated maintenance rather than simple plug-and-play updates.
StepSafe: Learning Timely Intervention with Context-Aware Safety Reasoning for Agentic Systems
arXiv AI
This work addresses a critical gap in agent safety: whether an action is safe depends on the sequence of prior actions, tool outputs, and interactions — not just the action itself. StepSafe provides step-level proactive risk prediction for agentic systems, a capability enterprises will need as they deploy agents that take consequential actions in real business processes.
JevAgentHarness: Decision Models That Share Control with LLMs — Cutting Agent Cost and Latency
arXiv AI
Researchers show that most LLM agent calls during task execution are simple multiple-choice decisions (like which tool to call next) that can be handled by smaller, cheaper 'decision models' rather than full LLM calls. This directly addresses 'token anxiety' — the cost and latency of running agents at scale — by potentially reducing compute costs for long-running agent tasks by an order of magnitude.
Kolab: Benchmarking Human-Agent Collaboration in Long-Horizon Workflows
arXiv AI
New research finds that strong performance on automation-focused AI benchmarks does not predict how well agents collaborate with humans on real long-horizon tasks. Humans value ongoing interaction capabilities (like revising plans) over single-attempt task completion. This challenges the prevailing enterprise assumption that the best benchmark-performing agent will deliver the most business value.
Athanor: An Agent-Native System for Autonomous Post-Training Research
arXiv AI
Researchers build Athanor, a system where frontier AI agents autonomously conduct post-training research on base models using real GPUs — a step toward recursive self-improvement where AI systems build stronger AI systems. While still in early stages, this represents a concrete demonstration of AI automating AI development, with significant implications for the pace of capability advancement.

AI Risk

The Self-Improvement Problem: Can automated gates reliably decide whether AI updates should be accepted?
arXiv AI
This research paper identifies a structural problem with AI self-improvement: any automated reviewer or reward model tasked with approving an AI's self-modifications faces fundamental limitations in reliably gatekeeping unsafe changes. As agentic systems increasingly modify their own code, prompts, and components, this finding has direct implications for enterprise AI governance frameworks and the safety of autonomous systems in production.
FlashAttention with Statistics: Low-Overhead Hallucination Detection
arXiv AI
Researchers demonstrate a method to detect AI hallucinations using attention-weight statistics without the quadratic memory cost of standard approaches. For enterprises deploying LLMs at scale, this represents progress on reducing the 'hallucination tax' — the hidden cost of AI errors — by enabling cheaper, more scalable automated quality checks on AI-generated content.
Counterfactual Neighborhood Risk Fields: Evidence-Grounded Safety for Vision-Language Models
arXiv AI
A new framework called CNRF provides evidence-grounded safety scoring for vision-language models, moving beyond simple harmful/benign classification to context-aware risk assessment. For enterprises deploying multimodal AI in sensitive domains like healthcare or content moderation, this approach could improve the reliability of safety guardrails and reduce liability exposure.
AgentPerturb: Frontier AI Agents Show Significant Robustness Gaps Under Real-World Perturbations
arXiv AI
A systematic evaluation framework reveals that frontier AI agents are significantly vulnerable to controlled perturbations mimicking real-world noise and complexity. The study quantifies robustness failures across current leading models and traces their mechanisms, providing enterprise risk managers with concrete evidence about the fragility of agentic AI in production environments.
When Reasoning Traces Conflict with Evidence: How Chain-of-Thought Can Override Facts
arXiv AI
Researchers find that reasoning traces retained at answer time can compete with and override current evidence when the two disagree, across GPT, Gemma, and Qwen model families. For enterprises relying on AI for evidence-based decisions, this reveals a mechanism by which AI systems can systematically ignore updated facts in favor of their prior reasoning — a form of institutional hallucination.

arXiv AI
A new benchmark reveals that in time-sensitive environments, faster but less capable AI models outperform more powerful but slower ones — GPT-6.1-sol beats Jev when games are paused but falls behind when they keep running during inference. For enterprises deploying AI in real-time applications (trading, operations, customer service), this finding suggests model selection should prioritize speed-to-decision, not just raw capability.

arXiv AI
Researchers find that high task accuracy and decodable intermediate answers in reasoning models do not prove the model is actually using chain-of-thought reasoning to reach its answer. This has enterprise implications: companies paying premium prices for 'reasoning' models may not be getting genuine reasoning capabilities, highlighting the need for more rigorous evaluation before deploying these models in high-stakes business decisions.
LLMs Still Fall Short of Conventional ML on EHR Prediction Tasks
arXiv AI
Controlled analysis shows that LLMs underperform conventional machine learning methods like decision trees on emergency department prediction tasks because they fail to select informative features and misjudge what numerical values imply for risk. This is a cautionary finding for healthcare executives rushing to replace proven ML systems with LLMs — the newer technology isn't universally superior.

Enterprise ROI

Tool-Augmented LLMs for Reliable Medical Calculation from Patient Notes
arXiv AI
Research demonstrates that LLMs alone are unreliable at extracting variables from clinical notes and executing medical calculations — a concrete example of the hallucination tax in healthcare. Tool-augmented approaches that pair LLMs with deterministic calculators significantly improve reliability, providing a blueprint for how enterprises in regulated industries should architect AI systems: LLMs for interpretation, specialized tools for precision.
Money-Mule Detection as a Relational Learning Problem
arXiv AI
Researchers demonstrate that AI-based relational learning significantly outperforms the rule-based systems currently used in production for detecting money-mule networks — accounts that launder criminal funds through many small, normal-looking transfers. For financial services executives, this study quantifies the detection gap between legacy compliance systems and modern AI approaches, with direct implications for fraud losses and regulatory exposure.
