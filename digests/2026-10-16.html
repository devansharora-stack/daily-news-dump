A new Harvard study found that more than half of male respondents claimed to recognize AI leaders who were completely fictional, revealing systematic overclaiming of AI expertise. For organizations, this finding has direct implications for AI hiring, strategy decisions, and boardroom credibility — suggesting companies should pressure-test claimed AI expertise with structured assessments rather than relying on self-reported knowledge.
Clinical documentation study shows small language models viable with verification architecture
arXiv
Researchers found that a 4B-parameter language model matched cloud-scale AI performance for clinical note generation when paired with a four-evaluator verification stack, focusing on patient safety by catching hallucinated lab values. This has significant enterprise implications: it suggests that smaller, cheaper AI models with robust verification layers may be sufficient for high-stakes applications, potentially reducing both cost and risk compared to deploying frontier models.
Research shows right-sizing AI models for agents can dramatically cut energy and cost per completed task
arXiv
A new paper demonstrates that per-call model benchmarks are misleading for agentic AI: because multi-step tasks only succeed if every step succeeds, small reliability differences per step compound into large task-completion gaps. The finding suggests enterprises running AI agents should evaluate total task cost and energy rather than per-inference metrics — directly relevant to the Token Anxiety challenge of running agents at scale.
AI peer review scores prove unreliable across reviewers, raising questions about AI-assisted scientific evaluation
arXiv
A study using 200 ICLR papers and approximately 14,000 AI-generated reviews found that individual AI review scores do not reliably reflect reviewer judgment, and revision improvements based on one AI's score are not consistently endorsed by other AI reviewers. For companies using AI for quality assurance, content evaluation, or decision support, this suggests single-model assessments may create a false sense of objectivity.
