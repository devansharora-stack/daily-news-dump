Stronger artificial intelligence breakthroughs hasten their own exhaustion
arXiv
Analysis of 7.2 million papers over 36 years finds that AI breakthroughs deplete 'diverse attention' — the pool of researchers exploring varied approaches — meaning stronger breakthroughs paradoxically accelerate their own diminishing returns. This challenges the assumption that more investment linearly accelerates AI progress, with implications for R&D spending and competitive strategy.
More memory, worse agents: Error reproduction and anti-persistence in LLM agents
arXiv
Research found that giving LLM agents more memory can actually degrade performance — stored abstractions and rules reproduce errors instead of preventing them. This counterintuitive finding has direct implications for enterprises building agent systems, challenging the assumption that more context and memory always improves AI agent reliability.
