Right Numbers, Wrong Story: When LLMs Narrate Financial Changes Beyond Their Inputs
arXiv AI
Researchers find that LLM-drafted financial reports frequently add unsupported judgments and causal explanations beyond their source data—a phenomenon they call 'unsupported framing'—far more often than human-written reports. This is a concrete hallucination risk for enterprises using AI for financial reporting, compliance documents, or investor communications.

arXiv AI
Researchers formally demonstrate that no guardrail can provide a general guarantee of safety for LLM outputs, arguing that promising perfection is 'an ethical failure of precision.' This has direct implications for enterprise risk management: organizations cannot rely solely on guardrails and must maintain human oversight and liability frameworks.
LLM agents leak private data through behavioral patterns in web interactions
arXiv AI
Researchers demonstrate that LLM agents' web browsing behaviors—searches, link clicks, page visits—create covert channels that expose users' private prompts, dialogue history, and connected data, even without direct network traffic analysis. Jagged Robustness: LLM safety degrades unevenly under quantization
arXiv AI
Researchers find that quantizing LLMs for efficient inference causes uneven degradation of safety properties—model safeguards and bias protections degrade while capabilities like truthfulness remain stable. This 'jagged robustness' means enterprises deploying compressed models for cost savings may unknowingly introduce safety and compliance risks.
